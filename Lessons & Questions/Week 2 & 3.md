### Refined Answer. I think science requires methodological naturalism as a method, but it does not logically require metaphysical materialism. ### Refined Answer. I think both better observations and better conceptual frameworks drive scientific progress, and they influence each other. Better observations and instruments can reveal phenomena that challenge existing ideas, while better conceptual frameworks allow scientists to interpret observations, formulate new questions and make predictions. For example, precise astronomical observations by Tycho Brahe helped Kepler develop mathematical laws of planetary motion, which later contributed to Newton's synthesis. ## How did Newtonian determinism change after statistical mechanics and quantum mechanics ?

### My Answer. At large scale level, newtonian mechanics work excellent, but at quantum level, quantum mechanics and statistical mechanics work better, so determinism changed in the sense that, it got shattered as many humans began to think that they have unlocked most of the secrets of universe.

### Refined Answer. Newtonian mechanics encouraged a strongly deterministic and mechanical picture of nature, in which the state of a system together with its laws could, in principle, determine its future state. Statistical mechanics introduced a different kind of description based on large numbers of particles and probabilistic quantities, while quantum mechanics challenged the classical deterministic picture at the fundamental level. However, this should not be reduced to simply saying that determinism was “shattered”: determinism and predictability are different concepts, and classical deterministic models can remain extremely useful within their domains.

### Refined Answer. I think the relationship between religious belief and scientific progress is more complicated than simply saying that religion and science are separate. Science uses empirical methods to investigate natural phenomena, whereas religious and metaphysical claims can concern questions that are not necessarily empirically testable. Therefore, the scientific method itself does not automatically prove or disprove religious metaphysical claims. Historically, religious beliefs and institutions have sometimes interacted with scientific development in different ways—supporting, shaping, constraining or motivating particular lines of inquiry depending on the context. So the relationship should be studied historically rather than reduced to “religion versus science.”

## Was the Scientific Revolution actually a single revolution, or a long heterogeneous transformation ?

### My Answer. Long heterogeneous transformation.

### Refined Answer. I think it was a long and heterogeneous transformation rather than a single sudden revolution. It involved multiple thinkers, methods and developments over an extended period, including changes in observation, measurement, experimentation, mathematics, induction, deduction and scientific institutions. Galileo, Bacon, Descartes, Kepler and Newton contributed in different but interconnected ways. Therefore, “Scientific Revolution” is useful as a historical label, but it should not make us imagine that modern science appeared suddenly from one event or followed a single linear path.
