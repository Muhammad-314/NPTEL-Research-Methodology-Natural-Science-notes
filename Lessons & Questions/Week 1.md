### Refined Answer. I think scientific inquiry genuinely moves toward objective truth, while producing increasingly useful and accurate models of reality. ## What exactly distinguishes methodological naturalism from metaphysical naturalism/materialism ?

### My Answer. I think main thing which distinguishes is beleif in metaphysical things (God, soul, ghost etc), while methodological naturalism tries to investigate, metaphysical naturalism & materialism doesn't as to the level former does.

### Refined Answer. Methodological naturalism is a methodological rule about how scientific investigation is conducted: it seeks explanations in terms of natural processes that can be empirically investigated. Metaphysical naturalism, in contrast, is a philosophical claim about reality itself—that reality ultimately consists only of the natural/physical world and that there is nothing supernatural. Materialism is a related metaphysical position that gives fundamental status to matter or the physical. Therefore, using natural explanations in science does not by itself prove metaphysical naturalism or materialism.

## How does modern causal inference improve upon Mill's methods ?

### My Answer. Honestly don't know.

### Refined Answer. Modern causal inference improves upon Mill's methods by providing more formal ways to reason about causation and deal with problems such as confounding, alternative causal structures, and counterfactuals. Mill's methods provide useful qualitative patterns for identifying possible causal factors through comparison, but they do not constitute a complete causal framework. Modern approaches use tools such as randomized experiments, counterfactual reasoning, causal graphs, potential outcomes, structural causal models, and methods for adjusting for confounding.

## Can causation be defined without reference to counterfactuals or interventions ?

### My Answer. Yes it can be, should it be? I say no.

### Refined Answer. Causation can be discussed or defined through approaches that do not explicitly rely on counterfactuals or interventions—for example, through mechanistic or process-based accounts. However, I think counterfactual and/or interventional reasoning provides a much stronger framework for distinguishing causation from mere association. Simply observing that A occurs before B, or that A and B systematically vary together, is not sufficient to establish that A causes B. Asking what would happen if A were changed or intervened upon helps make the causal claim more precise.

## When does a successful scientific theory deserve to be called "approximately true" ?

### My Answer. When it can objectively, gets close to what reality is actually & when if it is mathematical, it can give correct predictions & answers

### Refined Answer. A successful scientific theory deserves to be called approximately true when it captures important aspects or relationships of the underlying reality accurately within a specified domain, while possibly being incomplete or inaccurate outside that domain. Its ability to make successful predictions is important evidence for this, but predictive success alone is not necessarily sufficient. We should also consider whether the theory explains observed phenomena and whether its assumptions and mechanisms remain consistent with evidence across different conditions.

## How much can scientific objectivity be achieved when observation itself is theory-laden ?

### My Answer. When it can objectively, gets close to what reality is actually.

### Refined Answer. Scientific objectivity may never mean completely eliminating human subjectivity or theory-dependence from observation. Researchers inevitably bring assumptions into question selection, measurement, experimental design and interpretation. However, objectivity can be increased through procedures that constrain individual judgment, such as controlled experiments, blinding, randomization, replication, independent analysis, statistical methods and peer criticism. Therefore, I think scientific objectivity is better understood as a property of a rigorous process that allows evidence to constrain our beliefs, rather than as the complete absence of human subjectivity.

## Can multiple scientific models be simultaneously useful descriptions of the same phenomenon ?

### My Answer. I think it can.

### Refined Answer. Yes. Multiple scientific models can be simultaneously useful descriptions of the same phenomenon because they may operate at different levels, make different approximations, or be useful under different conditions. Their usefulness does not necessarily mean that all of them are equally complete or equally accurate. For example, Newtonian mechanics and relativistic mechanics can both successfully describe physical systems within different domains of applicability. Therefore, different models can coexist as useful representations of reality even when one provides a more comprehensive description.

## What is the precise relationship between evidence, explanation and prediction ?

### My Answer. Evidence is objective and reality unless it itself doesn't have any assumptions or subjectivity, whilst explanation is what might be the reason for evidence & might be for prediction, while prediction is answer or explanation or measure trying to match reality etc.

### Refined Answer. Evidence consists of observations or measurements that provide support for or against a scientific claim. An explanation is a proposed account of why or how the observed phenomenon occurs. A prediction is a specific consequence that an explanation or model implies should be observed under specified conditions. Evidence is then collected and compared with those predictions, allowing the explanation to be supported, challenged, refined or rejected. Thus, evidence constrains explanations, while explanations generate predictions that can produce new evidence.
