## Is Popperian falsifiability actually a sufficient criterion for distinguishing science from non-science ?

### My Answer. No, other than hypothesis, there are other auxillary assumptions & along with experiment design and other things to be looked at.

### Refined Answer. No. Popperian falsifiability is an important criterion for scientific hypotheses, but it is not by itself a sufficient criterion for distinguishing all science from non-science. If the predicted result fails, we cannot automatically conclude that \(H\) is false. The problem could lie in an auxiliary assumption, measurement procedure, implementation, experimental conditions, or statistical variation. Therefore, scientific evaluation also involves testability, empirical evidence, measurement quality, reproducibility/replicability, explanatory power, comparison with competing hypotheses, and methodological rigor.

## How exactly does the Duhem–Quine problem affect experimental hypothesis testing ?

### My Answer. Rather than just rejecting hypothesis, we've to look at other assumptions, design, etc.

### Refined Answer. A prediction may depend on: Main hypothesis + auxiliary assumptions + experimental conditions + measurement system → prediction. If the predicted result does not occur, we cannot immediately conclude that the main hypothesis is false. The failure could instead be caused by: an incorrect auxiliary assumption, faulty measurement, incorrect experimental conditions, an incorrect calculation, statistical fluctuation, or another assumption used to derive the prediction.

## What is the precise distinction between repeatability, reproducibility and replicability in different scientific disciplines ?

### My Answer. Able to repeat experiment is repeatibility with exact tools, with same location etc., while reproducibility means able to reproduce the experiment not nesseccarily, same location, same tools etc., replicability means able to replicate while not producing exact measures.

### Refined Answer. Repeatability: obtaining consistent results under essentially the same conditions — same procedure, observer, instrument, location, and relatively short time period. Reproducibility: obtaining consistent results when some measurement conditions are changed. However, in computational science, the term is often used differently: for example, obtaining consistent results using the same data, code, and computational workflow. Replicability: obtaining consistent results in a new study using new data while investigating the same scientific question.

## How should uncertainty be propagated through a multi-stage measurement pipeline ?

### My Answer. No idea.

### Refined Answer. When a final result depends on several measured quantities, the uncertainty in those inputs can contribute to uncertainty in the final result. Therefore, uncertainty should be propagated through the entire measurement pipeline, rather than reporting only the uncertainty of the final instrument. At every stage, we should ask: What are the uncertain inputs? How does each input affect the output? Are the uncertainties independent or correlated? Is there any systematic bias? How does the uncertainty change after each transformation?
